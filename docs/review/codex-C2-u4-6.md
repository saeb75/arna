| 22 — `c2-framing-the-question` | Teaching p1: “Before we start, let’s agree what we mean by X … proposing one is itself a move” | WRONG | The phrase proposes defining or agreeing on a definition; it does not itself propose a definition. | 23 — `c2-attacking-an-argument` | “That assumes our customers have cars.” | WRONG | A car park attracting more customers does not depend on existing customers owning cars. | 23 | “Cost is fixed, so the cheaper option is always better.” | WRONG | If cost is fixed, there cannot meaningfully be a cheaper option. The premise and conclusion are incoherent. Change the premise to “Quality is fixed” or change the conclusion. |
| 23 | “Even if crime fell, that doesn’t follow: other things changed too.” | WRONG | Grammatically, “that” can point to the immediately preceding claim that crime fell—the very fact the speaker accepts. General suggestions:

- The numerical coverage requirement is met: every teaching point has at least three items, and each multi-point lesson has two mixed items.
- Re-audit every situation item for socially reasonable alternatives, not merely answers that fail the intended label. A grammatical distractor can still violate single-answer grading if a real speaker might appropriately use it.
- Make role-play openings instantiate the exact discourse condition needed by the target: an undefined term, an established teasing relationship, an actual digression, or an anecdote that has already been told.
- Soften absolute pragmatic claims with “often,” “can,” or explicit conditions. At C2, tone, relationship, power, and shared context should be treated as variables rather than fixed meanings.
