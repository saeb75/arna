# GlotMate — A1 units 5, 6: practice block review

## Instructions for the reviewer

You are reviewing exercise content for **GlotMate**, an app that teaches English to adult beginners through conversation with a tutor avatar. Each lesson has three parts:

1. **Lecture** — the tutor explains the teaching points (shown below as "Teaching points").
2. **Practice block** — tap-based exercises, no keyboard (shown below). Every item has exactly ONE correct answer, graded by code (no AI).
3. **Role play** — free conversation with a character; the character's first line is shown below as "Role play openings" (one per learner context: everyday, work, travel, academic, exam).

**Exercise types:** Fill the gap (choose the word) · Arrange the words (build the sentence from tiles; extra tiles are distractors) · Read the situation, choose · Tap the wrong word · Complete the chat · Match the pairs · Listen and choose (audio plays, choose what you heard) · Listen and arrange · Say this sentence (speech recognition).

**Rules the content must follow:**
- **Exactly one correct answer.** No distractor may also be acceptable in the given context. This is the most important check.
- **Level A1.** Words and grammar a A1 learner can handle; only what the lesson teaches plus very common words.
- **Coverage:** each teaching point (p1, p2…) has at least 3 items; lessons with 2+ points end with at least 2 mixed ("mix") items.
- Distractors should be plausible learner mistakes, not nonsense.
- "Arrange" items: only one correct word order. "Listen and choose": options must sound different enough to tell apart.
- "Say this sentence": no names that speech recognition could misspell.
- "Tap the wrong word": only ONE word can fix the sentence (no subject–verb agreement errors, where changing the subject would also work).
- "Arrange the words": the first tile keeps its capital and the last tile keeps the final . ? ! — so a statement cannot be rebuilt as a question.
- Anything a drill tests (and every role play target phrase) must be taught in the teaching points.
- Role play openings: very simple, end with a question, and invite the learner to use the lesson's target phrases right away. The role play is voice only: an opening may ask about the learner's OWN life or things (they can imagine them), but never about a picture or object only the character can see.

**What to return:** a list of concrete problems only (skip items that are fine). For each: lesson id, the item (quote it), the problem, and your suggested fix. Use these categories: AMBIGUOUS (more than one correct answer), WRONG (the marked answer is not correct), LEVEL (too hard / unnatural for A1), WEAK (works but could teach better), COVERAGE (a teaching point is under-practised), OPENING (role play opening issue). End with any general suggestions.

## 29. I Love Cooking  `a1-i-love-cooking`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** like, love and hate followed by an -ing word · **Role play targets:** I love / I don't like / she enjoys cooking

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — I love cooking**: After enjoy, the next verb takes -ing. After like, love and hate, the -ing form is very common too: I love cooking. The strength goes: I hate it, I don't like it, I like it, I love it. _Examples:_ I love cooking. / She enjoys cooking too. / I hate getting up early.
- **p2 — I don't like**: For the negative, use 'don't like' or 'doesn't like' plus the -ing form. 'I don't mind' means it is OK for you; it is not a problem. _Examples:_ I don't like waiting. / He doesn't like flying. / I don't mind cooking.

**Practice block (11 items)**

1. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — She enjoys ___. → **✔ cooking** · cook · cooks
2. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — I love ___ in the sea. → **✔ swimming** · swim · swims
3. `p1` _Arrange the words_ — **She enjoys cooking for her friends.** (extra tiles: cook)
4. `p1` _Listen and choose_ — 🔊 "I hate getting up early." → **✔ I hate getting up early.** · I hate getting up late. · I hate get up early.
5. `p1` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **I love cooking.**
6. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — He doesn't like ___. → **✔ flying** · fly · flies
7. `p2` _Read the situation, choose_ — You are happy to cook. It is not a problem for you. What do you say? → **✔ I don't mind cooking.** · I don't like cooking. · I love cooking.
8. `p2` _Complete the chat_ — THEM: Do you like waiting? / YOU: ___ → **✔ No, I don't like waiting.** · No, I don't like wait. · No, I doesn't like waiting.
9. `p2` _Listen and arrange_ — 🔊 **My brother doesn't like flying.** (extra tiles: fly)
10. `mix` _Read the situation, choose_ — You love dancing, but running is not fun for you. What do you say? → **✔ I love dancing, but I don't like running.** · I love dance, but I don't like running. · I love dancing, but I don't like run.
11. `mix` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **I enjoy reading, but I don't like cooking.**

## 30. Do You Like It?  `a1-do-you-like-it`

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — me, him, her, it, us, them**: Use these words after a verb and after words like 'with' and 'about': I like it, she knows him, come with me, tell me about it. ## 32. Can You Help Me?  `a1-can-you-help-me`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** can for requests and permission · **Role play targets:** can you help / can I have / of course

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — Can you...?**: Use 'Can you' plus a verb to ask someone to do something for you. Adding 'please' makes the request friendlier. _Examples:_ Can you help me, please? / Can you open the window?
- **p2 — Can I...?**: Use 'Can I' to ask for permission or to ask for something you want. 'Could I' is a little more polite and works in the same way. _Examples:_ Can I have a glass of water? / Could I use your phone?

## 35. A Small Red Car  `a1-a-small-red-car`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** where descriptive words go: before a noun and after 'be' · **Role play targets:** it's very small / my new phone / really nice

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — a small red car**: Describing words come before the thing they describe: a small car, a red bag. They never change for plural: 'two small cars', not 'two smalls cars'. Size usually comes before colour: a small red car, a big blue box. ## 37. It's Cold Today  `a1-its-cold-today`

**Type:** phrases · **Focus:** talking about the weather and the seasons · **Role play targets:** it's cold / it's raining / what's the weather like
