# GlotMate — A2 units 1, 2: practice block review

## Instructions for the reviewer

You are reviewing exercise content for **GlotMate**, an app that teaches English to adult beginners through conversation with a tutor avatar. Each lesson has three parts:

1. **Lecture** — the tutor explains the teaching points (shown below as "Teaching points").
2. **Practice block** — tap-based exercises, no keyboard (shown below). Every item has exactly ONE correct answer, graded by code (no AI).
3. **Role play** — free conversation with a character; the character's first line is shown below as "Role play openings" (one per learner context: everyday, work, travel, academic, exam).

**Exercise types:** Fill the gap (choose the word) · Arrange the words (build the sentence from tiles; extra tiles are distractors) · Read the situation, choose · Tap the wrong word · Complete the chat · Match the pairs · Listen and choose (audio plays, choose what you heard) · Listen and arrange · Say this sentence (speech recognition).

**Rules the content must follow:**
- **Exactly one correct answer.** No distractor may also be acceptable in the given context. This is the most important check.
- **Level A2.** Words and grammar a A2 learner can handle; only what the lesson teaches plus very common words.
- **Coverage:** each teaching point (p1, p2…) has at least 3 items; lessons with 2+ points end with at least 2 mixed ("mix") items.
- Distractors should be plausible learner mistakes, not nonsense.
- "Arrange" items: only one correct word order. "Listen and choose": options must sound different enough to tell apart.
- "Say this sentence": no names that speech recognition could misspell.
- "Tap the wrong word": only ONE word can fix the sentence (no subject–verb agreement errors, where changing the subject would also work).
- "Arrange the words": the first tile keeps its capital and the last tile keeps the final . ? ! — so a statement cannot be rebuilt as a question.
- Anything a drill tests (and every role play target phrase) must be taught in the teaching points.
- Role play openings: very simple, end with a question, and invite the learner to use the lesson's target phrases right away. The role play is voice only: an opening may ask about the learner's OWN life or things (they can imagine them), but never about a picture or object only the character can see.

**What to return:** a list of concrete problems only (skip items that are fine). For each: lesson id, the item (quote it), the problem, and your suggested fix. Use these categories: AMBIGUOUS (more than one correct answer), WRONG (the marked answer is not correct), LEVEL (too hard / unnatural for A2), WEAK (works but could teach better), COVERAGE (a teaching point is under-practised), OPENING (role play opening issue). ## 1. While I Was Waiting  `a2-while-i-was-waiting`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** past continuous: affirmative and negative sentences · **Role play targets:** I was waiting / we were talking / it wasn't working

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — was / were + -ing**: Use 'was' with I, he, she and it, and 'were' with you, we and they. Add -ing to the verb: this says the action was in the middle of happening. _Examples:_ I was waiting outside the building. / We were talking about the film. / They were working late last night.
- **p2 — wasn't / weren't**: For the negative, put 'not' after was or were: wasn't, weren't. The verb keeps -ing and never changes. _Examples:_ The lift wasn't working yesterday. / We weren't listening to the radio.
- **p3 — at that moment**: A past time can identify a moment or a period: at seven o'clock, at that moment, all evening. The time expression can come at the start or at the end of the sentence. _Examples:_ At seven o'clock I was cooking dinner. / She was studying all evening.

**Practice block (12 items)**

1. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — We ___ talking about the film when you arrived. → **✔ were** · was · are
2. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — At eight o'clock last night she ___ cooking dinner. → **✔ was** · were · is
3. `p1` _Read the situation, choose_ — Yesterday at nine p.m. you were in the middle of watching a film. What do you say? → **✔ At nine I was watching a film.** · At nine I were watching a film. · At nine I was watch a film.
4. `p1` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **I was waiting outside the building.**
5. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — The lift ___ working yesterday, so we took the stairs. → **✔ wasn't** · weren't · didn't
6. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — We weren't ___ to the radio. → **✔ listening** · listen · listened
7. `p2` _Listen and arrange_ — 🔊 **It wasn't working this morning.** (extra tiles: weren't)
8. `p3` _Arrange the words_ — **She was studying all evening.** (extra tiles: were)
9. `p3` _Complete the chat_ — THEM: The lights went out at ten last night. / YOU: ___ → **✔ Really? At that moment I was reading in bed.** · Really? At that moment I were reading in bed. · Really? At that moment I was read in bed.
10. `p3` _Listen and choose_ — 🔊 "At seven o'clock I was cooking dinner." → **✔ At seven o'clock I was cooking dinner.** · At eleven o'clock I was cooking dinner. · At seven o'clock I was cleaning the kitchen.
11. `mix` _Read the situation, choose_ — Last night at ten your brother was working, but your sister was not. What do you say? → **✔ My brother was working, but my sister wasn't.** · My brother were working, but my sister wasn't. · My brother was working, but my sister weren't.

## 2. What Were You Doing?  `a2-what-were-you-doing`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** past continuous: questions · **Role play targets:** what were you doing / were you working / I was just

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — were you + -ing?**: To ask a question, put was or were before the person. The verb still takes -ing and 'did' is never used here. _Examples:_ Were you working late yesterday? / Was she waiting outside?
- **p2 — what were you doing?**: Question words come first: what, where, who, why. The order is question word, then was or were, then the person. _Examples:_ What were you doing at ten o'clock? / Where were you sitting?
- **p3 — short answers and 'just'**: Answer with was or were, not with did: Yes, I was. No, we weren't. Add 'just' when the action was small or ordinary. _Examples:_ Yes, I was. / No, we weren't. / I was just reading.

## 3. It Happened While…  `a2-it-happened-while`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** past simple and past continuous together, with when and while · **Role play targets:** while I was / when it happened / suddenly I heard

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — while I was ... , ... happened**: The long action in the background takes was or were plus -ing. The short action that cuts into it takes the past simple. _Examples:_ While I was cooking, the phone rang. / I was reading when the lights went out.
- **p2 — when and while**: 'While' usually introduces the longer background action. 'When' often introduces the shorter event, but it can also introduce a background action. Use a comma when the time clause comes before the main clause. _Examples:_ When it happened, I was standing in the kitchen. / While we were talking, she arrived.
- **p3 — suddenly I heard**: Words like suddenly and all of a sudden introduce the surprising short action. They usually come at the start of the clause. _Examples:_ Suddenly I heard a loud noise. / All of a sudden the door opened.

## 4. Ago, Last, Yesterday  `a2-ago-last-yesterday`

**Type:** phrases · **Focus:** expressions that say when something happened in the past · **Role play targets:** two years ago / last summer / about a month ago

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — ... ago**: Count back from now and put 'ago' at the end: two years ago, ten minutes ago. 'Ago' always follows the length of time, never comes before it. _Examples:_ I moved here two years ago. / She called me ten minutes ago.
- **p2 — last week / yesterday**: Use 'last' for the nearest past week, month, summer or year. Do not put 'in', 'on' or 'at' before last or yesterday: last summer, not in last summer. _Examples:_ We met last summer. / Last week I started a new course. / I saw him yesterday.
- **p3 — about a month ago**: Add 'about' or 'around' when you are not sure of the exact time. This is very common in conversation and sounds more natural than an exact date. _Examples:_ I saw him about a month ago. / It happened around ten years ago.

## 5. First, Then, After That  `a2-first-then-after-that`

**Type:** phrases · **Focus:** putting the events of a story in order · **Role play targets:** first of all / after that / in the end

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — first of all / to start with**: Open the story with the first event so the listener knows where it begins. You can also simply say 'First': First we got lost. These expressions usually come at the start of the sentence, followed by a comma. _Examples:_ First of all, we missed the train. / To start with, nobody answered the door.
- **p2 — then / after that / a bit later**: Move the story forward one step at a time with then, after that or a bit later. 'After that' looks back at the event you have just told. _Examples:_ Then we waited for an hour. / After that, the rain started.
- **p3 — in the end / finally**: Close the story with the result using in the end or finally. 'In the end' means after everything that happened, this was the result. _Examples:_ In the end we took a taxi. / Finally, everything was fine.

**Practice block (11 items)**

## 6. My Best Holiday  `a2-my-best-holiday`

**Type:** practice · **Focus:** telling the full story of a past trip · **Role play targets:** we stayed in / it was amazing / I'll never forget

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — we went to / we stayed in**: Say the place first and then where you stayed. Both verbs are in the past simple because the trip is finished. _Examples:_ We went to the mountains last spring. / We stayed in a small hotel near the beach.

## 8. I'm Going to Move  `a2-im-going-to-move`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** 'be going to' for plans and intentions · **Role play targets:** I'm going to / we're going to / she's going to

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — be going to + verb**: Use am, is or are with 'going to' and then the base verb. This form says the decision is already made, not that you are thinking about it. _Examples:_ I'm going to move to another city. / We're going to buy a bigger table.
- **p2 — she's going to**: Only am, is and are change with the person; 'going to' never changes. The verb after 'going to' never takes -s or -ing. _Examples:_ She's going to start a new job. / They're going to travel in July.
- **p3 — next month, soon**: A future time makes the plan clearer: next month, in the summer, soon. The time usually comes at the end of the sentence. _Examples:_ We're going to redecorate next month. / I'm going to call her soon.
