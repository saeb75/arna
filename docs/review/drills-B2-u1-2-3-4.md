## 1. If I Had Known  `b2-if-i-had-known`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** third conditional: setting up a past situation that did not happen · **Role play targets:** if I had known / had I known / if only I'd

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — if I had known**: The if-half uses the past perfect: had plus the past participle. It describes a past situation that was NOT true, so the real past was the opposite. _Examples:_ If I had known, I would have said something. / If we had left earlier, we wouldn't have missed it.
- **p2 — had I known**: You can drop 'if' and put 'had' before the subject: had I known. This inverted form is formal; it is more common in writing than in everyday speech. _Examples:_ Had I known, I would have acted differently. / Had we booked earlier, it would have been cheaper.
- **p3 — if only I'd**: 'If only' plus the past perfect expresses regret on its own, with no result half. 'I'd' here is short for 'I had', not 'I would'. _Examples:_ If only I'd asked for help. / If only we'd taken the earlier train.

**Practice block (11 items)**

1. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — If I ___ known about the meeting, I would have come. → **✔ had** · have · would
2. `p1` _Arrange the words_ — **If I had known, I would have called you.** (extra tiles: will)
3. `p1` _Tap the wrong word_ — If I had know about it, I would have told you. → wrong word: **know** → If I had known about it, I would have told you.
4. `p2` _Listen and choose_ — 🔊 "Had we booked earlier, it would have been cheaper." → **✔ Had we booked earlier, it would have been cheaper.** · We had booked early, so it was cheaper. · Have you booked yet? It might be cheaper.
5. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — ___ I known, I would have acted differently. → **✔ Had** · If · Would
6. `p2` _Arrange the words_ — **Had I known, I would have acted differently.** (extra tiles: knew)
7. `p3` _Complete the chat_ — THEM: You look upset. What's wrong? / YOU: ___ → **✔ I missed the last train. If only I'd left earlier.** · I missed the last train. If only I have left earlier. · I missed the last train. If only I had leave earlier.
8. `p3` _Fill the gap_ — If only we ___ taken the earlier train. → **✔ had** · have · would
9. `p3` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **If only I'd asked for help.**
10. `mix` _Read the situation, choose_ — You didn't know the shop closed early, so you arrived too late. You say what you would have done, and then add a short regret. What do you say? → **✔ If I had known, I would have gone earlier. If only I'd checked!** · If I had known, I would have gone earlier. If only I have checked! · If I would have known, I would have gone earlier. If only I'd checked!

## 2. I Would Have Called  `b2-i-would-have-called`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** third conditional: the result half of the sentence · **Role play targets:** I would have / we'd have / it would have been

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — I would have**: The result half uses would have plus the past participle. In this pattern the if-half uses had plus the past participle, not would. _Examples:_ I would have called you straight away. / She would have understood.
- **p2 — we'd have**: In speech 'would have' is usually contracted to 'would've' or 'we'd have'. It sounds like 'would of', but that spelling is never correct. _Examples:_ We'd have come if you had asked. / They'd have told us.
- **p3 — it would have been**: With 'be' the result half becomes 'would have been'. 'Could have' and 'might have' replace 'would have' when the result is less certain. _Examples:_ It would have been much easier. / It might have been worse.

**Practice block (11 items)**

## 3. I Should Have Listened  `b2-i-should-have-listened`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** should have and ought to have for regret and criticism · **Role play targets:** I should have / you shouldn't have / ought to have

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — I should have**: 'Should have' plus the past participle says the right thing was not done. It carries regret when you use it about yourself. _Examples:_ I should have listened to you. / I should have checked the times.
- **p2 — you shouldn't have**: 'Shouldn't have' criticises a past action, so it lands more softly than a direct accusation. In another context the same words thank someone for a gift. _Examples:_ You shouldn't have sent that email so quickly. / We shouldn't have left it so late.
- **p3 — ought to have**: 'Ought to have' means the same as 'should have' but sounds a little more formal. Unlike 'should', it keeps 'to' before 'have'. _Examples:_ We ought to have asked first. / They ought to have warned us.

**Practice block (11 items)**

## 4. If I Had Studied, I'd Be Rich Now  `b2-if-i-had-studied-id-be-rich-now`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** mixed conditional: a past cause with a present result · **Role play targets:** if I'd studied / I'd be / we would be now

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — if I'd studied**: The if-half stays in the past perfect, exactly as in the third conditional. 'I'd' here is short for 'I had'. _Examples:_ If I'd studied medicine, my life would look different. / If we'd bought that flat, we'd still be there.
- **p2 — I'd be**: The result half uses 'would' plus the base verb, with no 'have'. That missing 'have' is what moves the result into the present. _Examples:_ I'd be fluent by now. / She'd be much happier today.
- **p3 — we would be now**: Words like 'now' and 'today' make the present result explicit. Use the standard third conditional instead when the result also belongs to the past. _Examples:_ We would be living abroad now. / They would be finished by now. / I often wonder where we would be now.

## 5. Looking Back  `b2-looking-back`

**Type:** phrases · **Focus:** expressions for regret and hindsight · **Role play targets:** looking back / with hindsight / at the time I

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — looking back**: 'Looking back' opens a sentence and signals that a judgement is coming. It is usually followed by a comma and the whole reflection. _Examples:_ Looking back, it was the right decision. / Looking back, I was far too cautious.
- **p2 — with hindsight**: 'With hindsight' means knowing now what you did not know then. It sounds slightly more formal than 'looking back'. _Examples:_ With hindsight, we moved too fast. / With hindsight, the warning signs were there.
- **p3 — at the time I**: 'At the time' names what you believed then, before the reflection. Pairing it with 'looking back' shows the contrast between then and now. _Examples:_ At the time I thought it was hopeless. / At the time it felt like a disaster.

## 6. The Turning Point  `b2-the-turning-point`

**Type:** practice · **Focus:** talking about a single decision that changed your direction · **Role play targets:** that's when everything / it all changed / if that hadn't

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — that's when everything**: 'That's when everything changed' marks the exact turning point in a story. It usually comes after the event, not before it. _Examples:_ That's when everything changed for me. / That's when everything started to make sense.
- **p2 — it all changed**: 'It all changed after that' summarises the effect in one short sentence. Short sentences carry more weight after a long description. _Examples:_ It all changed after that conversation. / After that, it all changed very quickly.
- **p3 — if that hadn't**: 'If that hadn't happened' looks back at how differently things could have gone. It takes the past perfect, like any unreal past condition. _Examples:_ If that hadn't happened, I would still be there. / If that hadn't come up, I'd have stayed.

## 7. What I'd Do Differently  `b2-what-id-do-differently`

**Type:** practice · **Focus:** reflecting on a mistake and what you learned from it · **Role play targets:** what I'd change / next time I'd / I'd never do that

- **p3 — I'd never do that**: 'I'd never do that again' closes the reflection firmly. 'Again' is what makes it about repeating, not about the original act. _Examples:_ I'd never do that again. / I'd never leave it that late again.

## 8. It Might Have Been Worse  `b2-it-might-have-been-worse`

## 25. Make, Let, Help  `b2-make-let-help`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** making someone do something and allowing them to · **Role play targets:** they made me / she let me / he helped me

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — they made me**: 'Make someone do something' takes the base verb with no 'to'. In the passive, 'to' comes back: I was made to wait. _Examples:_ They made me wait an hour. / She made us rewrite the whole thing.
- **p2 — she let me**: 'Let someone do something' also takes the base verb with no 'to'. 'Allow' means the same but takes 'to': she allowed me to leave. _Examples:_ She let me leave early. / They let us take photographs.
- **p3 — he helped me**: 'Help' works with or without 'to': he helped me carry it, or to carry it. The version without 'to' is more common in speech. _Examples:_ He helped me carry the boxes. / She helped me to understand it.

## 26. Arranging Services  `b2-arranging-services`

**Teaching points**
