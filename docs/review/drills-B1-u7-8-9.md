1. **Lecture** — the tutor explains the teaching points (shown below as "Teaching points").

**Rules the content must follow:**

- **Level B1.** Words and grammar a B1 learner can handle; only what the lesson teaches plus very common words.

## 37. The Woman Who Called  `b1-the-woman-who-called`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** joining sentences with who, which and that to identify someone · **Role play targets:** people who / something that / anyone who wants

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — who**: Use who to join sentences and identify or describe people. Who replaces the person in the second sentence. _Examples:_ She is the person who helps everyone. / People who speak two languages are bilingual. / Anyone who wants to come is welcome.
- **p2 — which and that**: Use which or that to join sentences and identify or describe things. Which and that replace the thing in the second sentence. That can often replace which in these sentences. _Examples:_ This is something that makes life easier. / I want a phone which takes good photos.

**Practice block (12 items)**

1. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — The woman ___ called you is my aunt. → **✔ who** · which · where
2. `p1` _Arrange the words_ — **She is the woman who called me.** (extra tiles: which)
3. `p1` _Tap the wrong word_ — My mum is the person which taught me to cook. → wrong word: **which** → My mum is the person who taught me to cook.
4. `p1` _Listen and choose_ — 🔊 "Anyone who wants a ticket can ask me." → **✔ Anyone who wants a ticket can ask me.** · Anyone who has a ticket can come in. · Everyone wants a ticket for the show.
5. `p1` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **People who travel a lot learn quickly.**
6. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — Do you have a bag ___ is big enough for my laptop? → **✔ that** · who · where
7. `p2` _Arrange the words_ — **This is the key that opens the door.** (extra tiles: who)
8. `p2` _Read the situation, choose_ — You are describing a thing in your kitchen. It makes coffee very fast. What do you say? → **✔ It's a machine which makes coffee very fast.** · It's a machine who makes coffee very fast. · It's a machine which make coffee very fast.
9. `p2` _Tap the wrong word_ — The milk who was in the fridge has gone bad. → wrong word: **who** → The milk that was in the fridge has gone bad.
10. `p2` _Complete the chat_ — THEM: What kind of films do you like? / YOU: ___ → **✔ I like films that make me laugh.** · I like films who make me laugh. · I like films that makes me laugh.
11. `mix` _Complete the chat_ — THEM: Who is that man? / YOU: ___ → **✔ He's the man who fixed the car that broke down.** · He's the man which fixed the car who broke down. · He's the man who fixed the car who broke down.

## 38. My Brother, Who Lives Abroad  `b1-my-brother-who-lives-abroad`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** adding extra information with commas around the added part · **Role play targets:** which was lovely / who happens to be

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — commas around extra information**: Use commas to separate extra information from the main part of the sentence. The extra information is not necessary to identify the person or thing. The sentence is still correct if you remove the part between commas. If the extra information comes at the end of the sentence, use one comma before it. _Examples:_ My brother, who lives in London, is coming to visit. / The cake, which was lovely, disappeared quickly. / I visited my brother, who lives abroad.
- **p2 — who/which + extra detail**: Use 'who' for people and 'which' for things when adding extra information. Do not use 'that' for extra information between commas: say 'The cake, which was lovely,' not 'The cake, that was lovely,'. 'Who happens to be' adds a fact that is true by chance: My neighbour, who happens to be a nurse, helped me. _Examples:_ My friend, who happens to be a doctor, helped me. / The park, which was lovely, was full of people.

**Practice block (11 items)**

1. `p1` _Read the situation, choose_ — You have only one brother. He lives in Canada and he is a chef. Which sentence is correct? → **✔ My brother, who lives in Canada, is a chef.** · My brother who lives in Canada, is a chef. · My brother, who lives in Canada is a chef.
2. `p1` _Listen and arrange_ — 🔊 **My sister, who lives in Spain, is coming tomorrow.** (extra tiles: which)
3. `p1` _Read the situation, choose_ — Take the extra information out of this sentence: 'My car, which is very old, still works.' What is left? → **✔ My car still works.** · My car is very old. · Which is very old still works.
4. `p1` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **My brother, who lives abroad, calls me every week.**
5. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — Our new office, ___ is near the station, is very bright. → **✔ which** · who · that
6. `p2` _Tap the wrong word_ — My uncle, which happens to be a cook, made dinner. → wrong word: **which** → My uncle, who happens to be a cook, made dinner.
7. `p2` _Complete the chat_ — THEM: How was your holiday? / YOU: ___ → **✔ Our hotel, which was lovely, was right by the sea.** · Our hotel, who was lovely, was right by the sea. · Our hotel, which were lovely, was right by the sea.

## 39. The Place Where We Met  `b1-the-place-where-we-met`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** using where, when and whose to join sentences · **Role play targets:** where we met / whose name is / when it happened

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — where**: Use where to join sentences about a place. Put 'where' after a place word (house, cafe, town) and then say what happens or happened there. _Examples:_ This is the cafe where we met. / I remember the house where I grew up.
- **p2 — when**: Use when to join sentences about a time or date. Put 'when' after a time word (day, year, season) and then say what happens or happened at that time. _Examples:_ That was the day when it happened. / Summer is the season when we travel.
- **p3 — whose**: Use whose to join sentences about a person and something they have or own. 'Whose' replaces his, her or their: I met a man. His car is red. = I met a man whose car is red. _Examples:_ She is the friend whose name is Anna. / I met a man whose car is red.

## 40. A Nice Old Wooden Table  `b1-a-nice-old-wooden-table`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** the order describing words go in before a thing · **Role play targets:** nice old / small round / big red one

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — adjective order before a noun**: When using more than one describing word before a noun, the usual order is opinion, size, age, shape, colour, then material, then the noun. Say 'nice old', not 'old nice'; say 'big red', not 'red big'. Use 'one' after describing words if you do not repeat the noun. _Examples:_ I like that nice old table. / She wants the big red one.
- **p2 — what is it made of**: Material words come last, closest to the noun: a wooden table, a leather bag. A material word almost never takes 'very' or a comparative form. _Examples:_ It's a nice old wooden table. / She bought a small round metal tray.

## 41. Hard to Describe  `b1-hard-to-describe`

**Type:** phrases · **Focus:** describing something when you do not know the exact word · **Role play targets:** it's a kind of / sort of like / what do you call

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — it's a kind of**: Use 'it's a kind of' + a general word (tool, food, machine) when you do not know the exact name of something. This phrase introduces a general category or type. _Examples:_ It's a kind of tool for tightening screws. / It's a kind of tape, but stronger.
- **p2 — sort of like**: Use 'sort of like' to compare the unknown item to something familiar. This phrase helps the listener understand by giving a similar example. _Examples:_ It's sort of like a hammer, but smaller. / It's sort of like glue, but for metal.
- **p3 — what do you call**: Use 'what do you call' to ask for the correct word when you do not know it. This phrase is often used before describing the item. _Examples:_ What do you call the thing you use to measure angles? / What do you call it when you want to join two pipes together?

## 42. Someone I Admire  `b1-someone-i-admire`

**Type:** practice · **Focus:** describing a person in detail and saying why they matter to you · **Role play targets:** what I admire / he's the kind of / she always used to

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — what I admire**: 'What I admire' introduces a quality or action you respect in someone. Use it to highlight something special about a person. _Examples:_ What I admire about him is his honesty. / What I admire most is her patience.
- **p2 — he's the kind of**: 'He's the kind of' describes the type of person someone is. Use it to introduce a typical behavior or attitude. _Examples:_ He's the kind of person who always helps others. / She's the kind of friend you can trust.

## 43. She Must Be Tired  `b1-she-must-be-tired`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** must, might and can't for working out what is true now · **Role play targets:** she must be / it can't be / he might be

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — must + be/verb**: Use 'must' plus be or a base verb when the evidence makes you strongly believe something is true. Do not use to after must. Use must for strong logical guesses about the present. _Examples:_ She must be at work now. / He must know the answer.
- **p2 — might + be/verb**: Use might plus be or a base verb to say something is possible now, but you are not sure. Do not use to after might. Use might for weak or possible guesses about the present. _Examples:_ He might be busy right now. / She might know the answer.
- **p3 — can't + be/verb**: Use 'can't' plus be or a base verb when the evidence makes something seem impossible. Do not use to after can't. Use can't for strong logical guesses that something is impossible now. _Examples:_ It can't be true. / She can't be serious.

## 54. Then and Now  `b1-then-and-now`

**Type:** practice · **Focus:** describing how your own life has changed over the years · **Role play targets:** back in those days / now I tend to / so much has changed

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — back in those days**: Use 'back in those days' to talk about a time in the past, often many years ago. This phrase introduces a memory or description of how things used to be. _Examples:_ Back in those days, I used to play outside every afternoon. / Back in those days, we did not have smartphones.
