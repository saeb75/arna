## 38. How Much Sugar?  `a2-how-much-sugar`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** much, many and 'a lot of' with things you can and cannot count · **Role play targets:** how much / how many / not much

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — how much / how many**: Use 'how many' with things you can count: apples, eggs, people. Use 'how much' with things you cannot count: sugar, water, time, money. _Examples:_ How much sugar do we need? / How many eggs are left?
- **p2 — a lot of**: 'A lot of' works with both kinds and is very common in positive sentences. 'Much' and 'many' sound more natural in questions and negatives. _Examples:_ There's a lot of milk in the fridge. / We have a lot of plates.
- **p3 — not much / not many**: The negative keeps the same choice: not much sugar, not many eggs. 'None' means zero and stands alone as an answer. _Examples:_ There isn't much bread left. / There aren't many clean cups.

**Practice block (12 items)**

1. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — How ___ eggs are left? → **✔ many** · much · a lot
2. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — How ___ money do you have? → **✔ much** · many · lot
3. `p1` _Read the situation, choose_ — You want to know the number of people at the party. What do you ask? → **✔ How many people are coming?** · How much people are coming? · How many people is coming?
4. `p1` _Listen and choose_ — 🔊 "How much sugar do we need?" → **✔ How much sugar do we need?** · How much sugar do we have? · How much water do we need?
5. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — There's ___ milk in the fridge. → **✔ a lot of** · many · a lot
6. `p2` _Arrange the words_ — **We have a lot of plates.** (extra tiles: much)
7. `p2` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **We have a lot of time today.**
8. `p3` _Fill the gap_ — There isn't ___ bread left. → **✔ much** · many · many of
9. `p3` _Complete the chat_ — THEM: Have we got any eggs? / YOU: ___ → **✔ Not many, only two.** · Not much, only two. · Not many, only a two.

## 39. A Few or a Little?  `a2-a-few-or-a-little`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** 'a few' and 'a little' for small amounts · **Role play targets:** just a few / only a little / quite a lot

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — just a few**: 'A few' goes with things you can count: a few days, a few people. 'Just a few' makes the small amount sound deliberate rather than disappointing. _Examples:_ It only takes a few minutes. / I invited just a few friends.
- **p2 — only a little**: 'A little' goes with things you cannot count: a little time, a little money. 'Only a little' stresses that the amount is small. _Examples:_ I have only a little time today. / Add a little salt at the end.
- **p3 — quite a lot**: 'Quite a lot' means a fairly large amount and works with both countable and uncountable nouns. 'A few' and 'a little' are positive; 'few' and 'little' alone sound negative. _Examples:_ It costs quite a lot. / We still have quite a lot of time.

## 40. Too Small, Not Big Enough  `a2-too-small-not-big-enough`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** 'too' before and 'enough' after a describing word · **Role play targets:** it's too small / not big enough / too expensive

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — it's too small**: 'Too' comes before the describing word and means more than you want. When 'too' means more than wanted or suitable, it describes a problem: too big, too noisy. _Examples:_ This jacket is too small for me. / The room was too noisy to work in.
- **p2 — not big enough**: 'Enough' comes after the describing word: big enough, warm enough. Before a noun the order changes: enough time, enough chairs. _Examples:_ The table isn't big enough for six. / We don't have enough chairs.
- **p3 — too expensive**: 'Too ... to ...' gives the consequence: too expensive to buy. 'Enough to' works the same way: warm enough to sit outside. _Examples:_ It's too expensive to buy today. / It's warm enough to sit outside.

**Practice block (13 items)**

3. `p1` _Read the situation, choose_ — The shoes are size 38, but you need size 40. What do you say? → **✔ They're too small.** · They're small enough. · They're too big.
4. `p1` _Listen and choose_ — 🔊 "It's too cold to swim." → **✔ It's too cold to swim.** · It's too cold to sleep. · It's warm enough to swim.
5. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — The table isn't big ___ for six people. → **✔ enough** · too · so
6. `p2` _Arrange the words_ — **We don't have enough chairs.** (extra tiles: too)
7. `p2` _Complete the chat_ — THEM: Can we sit outside? / YOU: ___ → **✔ No, it isn't warm enough.** · No, it isn't enough warm. · No, it isn't warm too.
8. `p2` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **The room isn't big enough.**

## 41. How Long, How Far  `a2-how-long-how-far`

**Type:** phrases · **Focus:** asking about time, distance and size · **Role play targets:** how long does it / how far is / about ten kilometres

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — how long does it take**: 'How long does it take?' asks about duration, not about length. The answer usually starts with 'it takes' plus a period of time. _Examples:_ How long does it take to get there? / It takes about forty minutes.
- **p2 — how far is**: 'How far' asks about distance and is usually answered in metres, kilometres or miles. 'How big' and 'how deep' follow the same pattern. _Examples:_ How far is the station from here? / How big is the room?
- **p3 — about ten kilometres**: 'About', 'around' and 'roughly' make an answer approximate. Exact numbers are rarely needed in everyday conversation. _Examples:_ It's about ten kilometres. / Roughly half an hour by bus.

**Practice block (11 items)**

4. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — How ___ is the station from here? → **✔ far** · long · much
5. `p2` _Read the situation, choose_ — You want to know the distance to the beach. What do you ask? → **✔ How far is the beach?** · How long is the beach? · How much is the beach?

## 42. Something, Anything, Nothing  `a2-something-anything-nothing`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** words built with some-, any- and no- · **Role play targets:** something to eat / anything else / nothing special

**Teaching points**

## 43. Mine, Yours, Theirs  `a2-mine-yours-theirs`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** possessive pronouns and asking with 'whose' · **Role play targets:** it's mine / that's yours / whose is this

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — it's mine**: Possessive pronouns stand alone: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs. They replace the noun, so nothing follows them. _Examples:_ That bag is mine. / The blue one is hers.
- **p2 — my / mine**: 'My' comes before a noun; 'mine' stands alone. Say 'my coat' or 'mine', never 'mine coat'. _Examples:_ This is my coat. / This coat is mine.
- **p3 — whose is this**: 'Whose' asks who owns something. It sounds the same as 'who's' but has a different meaning. _Examples:_ Whose is this jacket? / Whose keys are these?

## 44. Packing for a Trip  `a2-packing-for-a-trip`

**Type:** practice · **Focus:** deciding what to take and how much of it you need · **Role play targets:** I don't need / we should take / that's enough

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — I don't need / that's enough**: 'I don't need' is followed by a noun or by 'to' plus a verb. It is softer than 'I won't take' when you are deciding together. ## 45. Because and So  `a2-because-and-so`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** joining two ideas with because and so · **Role play targets:** because I was / so I decided / that's why

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — because I was**: 'Because' introduces the reason and answers the question why. The reason can come first or second, but the two parts stay in one sentence. _Examples:_ I left early because I was tired. / Because it was raining, we stayed in.
- **p2 — so I decided**: 'So' introduces the result of what came before. The order matters: reason first, then 'so', then result. _Examples:_ The train was cancelled, so I decided to walk. / It was late, so we went home.
- **p3 — that's why**: 'That's why' points back to the reason you have just given. It usually starts a new sentence. _Examples:_ The road was closed. That's why I was late. / That's why I called you.

**Practice block (13 items)**

1. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — Why did you leave early? I left early ___ I was tired. → **✔ because** · so · that's why

4. `p1` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **I left early because I was tired.**
