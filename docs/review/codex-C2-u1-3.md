| 2–21 — academic role plays | Examples: “When does understatement stop working?” / “Why does a pitch fail without an ask?” / “Why must the condition come in the same breath?” | OPENING | Nearly all academic openings ask for metalinguistic explanation. Learners can answer them without producing any role-play target. Rewrite them as short scenarios that naturally require the exact phrases, then let the character ask follow-up analytical questions. |

General suggestions:

- Design every distractor against the “C2 defence” test: irony, cordial implication, and pragmatic appropriateness can make a superficially inferior option valid.
- Give all facts needed to choose among pragmatic alternatives—especially exact counts, known uncertainty, causal links, and whose interests are being represented.
- Replace categorical teaching claims with *can*, *may*, or explicit contextual conditions unless the claim is genuinely grammatical rather than pragmatic.
- Make role-play openings elicit production, not recognition or explanation. The first learner turn should naturally accommodate at least one exact target phrase.
- Several drills test only phrase form. Add more contrastive situations where the learner must decide when the phrase would be misleading, excessive, or inappropriate.
