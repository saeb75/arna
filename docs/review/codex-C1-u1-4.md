### General suggestions

- Replace elementary or nonsensical distractors such as `striked`, `standed`, `enought`, `panic have`, `an one`, `Readed`, `everythings`, and `I’m be`. At C1, distractors should be plausible errors involving register, scope, inversion, ellipsis, collocation, or discourse meaning.
- In lessons 17–19, consistently distinguish logical consequence, pragmatic suggestion, and attributed intention. Interpretations should be hedged unless the context makes them unavoidable.
- Rework conceptual tutor openings into short, concrete scenarios. The learner’s first natural response should contain a target phrase, not an explanation of the grammar or rhetoric.
- Avoid treating a preferred or neutral word order as the only grammatical order. When testing form, use distractors that remain wrong under contrastive stress, regional usage, and informal speech.
- Equivalence questions should use complete sentences with an explicit timeline and consequence; isolated fragments such as `but for you` are too context-dependent.
