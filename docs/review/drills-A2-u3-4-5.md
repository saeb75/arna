## 16. Bigger and Better  `a2-bigger-and-better`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** comparing two things with short words plus -er and than · **Role play targets:** bigger than / cheaper than / much better

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — -er than**: Add -er to short describing words and put 'than' before the second thing. For many one-syllable words ending consonant + vowel + consonant, double the final consonant: big becomes bigger, hot becomes hotter. _Examples:_ This flat is bigger than the other one. / The second one is cheaper than the first.
- **p2 — y becomes -ier**: Words ending in -y change the y to -ier: easy becomes easier, busy becomes busier. The rest of the sentence does not change. _Examples:_ The journey is easier than I expected. / This street is noisier than ours.
- **p3 — much better**: Put 'much', 'a lot' or 'a bit' before the comparative to say how big the difference is. 'Good' has an irregular comparative: better, not gooder. _Examples:_ It's much better than the old one. / This one is a bit smaller.

**Practice block (12 items)**

1. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — This flat is ___ than the other one. → **✔ bigger** · biger · more big
2. `p1` _Arrange the words_ — **It's cheaper than the other one.** (extra tiles: cheap)
3. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — Today is ___ than yesterday. → **✔ hotter** · hoter · more hot
4. `p1` _Listen and choose_ — 🔊 "My room is smaller than yours." → **✔ My room is smaller than yours.** · My room is bigger than yours. · My room is smaller than his.
5. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — The test was ___ than I expected. → **✔ easier** · easyer · more easy
6. `p2` _Tap the wrong word_ — This street is noisyer than ours. → wrong word: **noisyer** → This street is noisier than ours.
7. `p2` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **The journey is easier than I expected.**
8. `p3` _Fill the gap_ — It's ___ better than the old one. → **✔ much** · more · very
9. `p3` _Read the situation, choose_ — The new phone is very good. The old one was bad. What do you say? → **✔ The new one is much better.** · The new one is much gooder. · The new one is very better.
10. `p3` _Listen and arrange_ — 🔊 **This one is a bit smaller.** (extra tiles: smallest)
11. `mix` _Complete the chat_ — THEM: Which flat do you like? / YOU: ___ → **✔ The second one. It's much bigger and easier to find.** · The second one. It's much biger and easier to find. · The second one. It's very bigger and easier to find.
12. `mix` _Read the situation, choose_ — Bus: 2 euros, 40 minutes. Train: 5 euros, 20 minutes. What is true? → **✔ The bus is a lot cheaper, but the train is faster.** · The bus is a lot cheapper, but the train is faster. · The bus is a lot more cheap, but the train is faster.

**Role play openings**

- everyday: Nadia (a friend helping you decide) — "You saw two flats today. Which one is bigger?"
- work: Ryan (a colleague looking at two offices) — "We can have an office on the third floor or the fifth. Which one is better for you?"
- travel: Beatrix (a travel agent) — "One hotel is near the beach, one is in the centre. Which one is better for you?"

## 17. More Interesting Than  `a2-more-interesting-than`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** comparing with 'more' and the common irregular comparisons · **Role play targets:** more expensive than / less crowded / worse than

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — more ... than**: Many longer describing words take 'more' instead of -er. Some two-syllable words still take -er, especially those ending in -y: happy becomes happier. Never use both together: 'more expensive', not 'more expensiver'. _Examples:_ This city is more expensive than my hometown. / The museum is more interesting than the castle.
- **p2 — less ... than**: 'Less' is the opposite of 'more' and works the same way. 'Less crowded' means fewer people than the other place. _Examples:_ The old town is less crowded in winter. / This route is less tiring than the other one.
- **p3 — better and worse**: 'Good' and 'bad' are irregular: they become better and worse. These two never take -er or 'more'. _Examples:_ The weather here is better than at home. / The traffic was worse than yesterday.

**Practice block (12 items)**

1. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — This city is ___ than my hometown. → **✔ more expensive** · expensiver · more expensiver
2. `p1` _Tap the wrong word_ — The museum is more interestinger than the castle. → wrong word: **interestinger** → The museum is more interesting than the castle.
3. `p1` _Arrange the words_ — **It's more comfortable than my old flat.** (extra tiles: most)
4. `p1` _Listen and choose_ — 🔊 "It's more expensive than the bus." → **✔ It's more expensive than the bus.** · It's less expensive than the bus. · It's more expensive than the boat.
5. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — The old town is ___ crowded in winter. → **✔ less** · fewer · lesser
6. `p2` _Read the situation, choose_ — In the morning there are 50 people in the gym. In the evening there are 200. What do you say? → **✔ The gym is less crowded in the morning.** · The gym is fewer crowded in the morning. · The gym is less crowdeder in the morning.
7. `p2` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **The museum is less crowded on Mondays.**
8. `p3` _Fill the gap_ — The traffic was ___ than yesterday. → **✔ worse** · badder · more bad

## 18. The Best One  `a2-the-best-one`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** superlatives for the top or bottom of a group · **Role play targets:** it's the best / that's the cheapest / my favourite one

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — the -est**: Short words take the -est and almost always need 'the' before them. The spelling rules are the same as the comparative: big becomes the biggest, easy becomes the easiest. _Examples:_ That's the cheapest one in the shop. / This is the easiest way to get there.
- **p2 — the most**: Longer words take 'the most' instead of the -est. 'Good' and 'bad' are irregular here too: the best and the worst. _Examples:_ It's the most comfortable chair in the office. / That was the best meal of the trip.
- **p3 — my favourite one**: 'My favourite' says which one you like most without a comparison word. Add 'because' to give the reason for your choice. _Examples:_ This is my favourite one because it is light. / The small one is my favourite.

**Practice block (12 items)**

3. `p1` _Arrange the words_ — **This hotel is the cheapest.** (extra tiles: cheapests)
4. `p1` _Listen and choose_ — 🔊 "It's the longest day of the year." → **✔ It's the longest day of the year.** · It's the shortest day of the year. · It's the longest day of the week.
5. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — It's ___ comfortable chair in the office. → **✔ the most** · the more · most
6. `p2` _Read the situation, choose_ — You ate five meals on your trip. One was better than all the others. What do you say? → **✔ That was the best meal of the trip.** · That was the goodest meal of the trip. · That was the most good meal of the trip.

8. `p3` _Fill the gap_ — This is my ___ one because it's light. → **✔ favourite** · favourites · the favourite

## 19. I Prefer This One  `a2-i-prefer-this-one`

**Type:** phrases · **Focus:** saying which of two things you prefer and why · **Role play targets:** I prefer / I like this one / I'd rather have

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — I prefer**: 'Prefer' can be followed by a noun, an -ing form or a to-infinitive. When you compare two activities directly, use 'to': I prefer walking to driving. _Examples:_ I prefer the blue one. / I prefer walking to driving. / I prefer to travel by train.
- **p2 — I like this one**: 'This one' and 'that one' avoid repeating the name of the thing. 'Better' turns a simple opinion into a comparison: I like this one better. _Examples:_ I like this one better. / I like the smaller one.
- **p3 — I'd rather have**: 'I'd rather' is followed by the base verb, with no 'to'. It sounds slightly softer than 'I want'. _Examples:_ I'd rather have the black one. / I'd rather pay a bit more for a good one.

**Practice block (11 items)**

1. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — I prefer tea ___ coffee. → **✔ to** · than · from

3. `p1` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **I prefer to travel by train.**
4. `p2` _Complete the chat_ — THEM: Do you want the red bag or the blue one? / YOU: ___ → **✔ I like the blue one better.** · I like the blue one more better. · I like the blue one gooder.
5. `p2` _Arrange the words_ — **I like the smaller one.** (extra tiles: ones)
6. `p2` _Listen and choose_ — 🔊 "I like that one better." → **✔ I like that one better.** · I like that one less. · I like that colour better.
7. `p3` _Fill the gap_ — I'd rather ___ the black one. → **✔ have** · to have · having
8. `p3` _Read the situation, choose_ — You don't want the cheap one. You want to pay more for a good one. What do you say? → **✔ I'd rather pay a bit more.** · I'd rather to pay a bit more. · I'd rather paying a bit more.

What do you say? → **✔ I like the green one better, but I'd rather pay less.** · I like the green one better, but I'd rather to pay less. · I like the green one more better, but I'd rather pay less.

## 20. As Cheap As  `a2-as-cheap-as`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** 'as … as' and 'not as … as' for saying things are equal or not · **Role play targets:** as good as / not as expensive / just as nice

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — as ... as**: Put the plain describing word between the two 'as' words, with no -er and no 'more'. This says the two things are the same in that way. _Examples:_ This one is as good as the expensive one. / The train is as fast as the bus.
- **p2 — not as ... as**: 'Not as ... as' means the first thing is less than the second. 'It's not as expensive as that one' means it costs less. _Examples:_ It's not as expensive as I thought. / Today is not as cold as yesterday.
- **p3 — just as nice**: 'Just as' makes the equality stronger: exactly the same, not almost. 'Almost as' says it is close but slightly less. _Examples:_ The cheaper one is just as nice. / This route is almost as quick.

**Practice block (11 items)**

3. `p1` _Arrange the words_ — **My sister is as tall as me.** (extra tiles: taller)
4. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — It's not ___ expensive as I thought. → **✔ as** · more · than
5. `p2` _Read the situation, choose_ — Today it is 15 degrees. Yesterday it was 5 degrees. What do you say? → **✔ Today is not as cold as yesterday.** · Today is not as cold than yesterday. · Today is not as colder as yesterday.

7. `p3` _Read the situation, choose_ — The cheap bag is exactly as nice as the expensive bag. What do you say? → **✔ The cheap one is just as nice.** · The cheap one is almost as nice. · The cheap one is just as nicer.

## 21. Absolutely Freezing!  `a2-absolutely-freezing`

**Type:** phrases · **Focus:** extreme describing words like freezing, huge and tiny · **Role play targets:** absolutely freezing / it was huge / really tiny

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — freezing / boiling / huge / tiny**: Strong words already contain 'very': freezing means very cold, huge means very big. Because of that, we do not usually say 'very freezing'. _Examples:_ It was absolutely freezing outside. / Their kitchen is tiny.
- **p2 — absolutely / really**: Use 'absolutely' before strong words instead of 'very'. 'Really' works with both strong and plain words. _Examples:_ The room was absolutely boiling. / The garden was really tiny.

## 22. He Drives Carefully  `a2-he-drives-carefully`

**Type:** grammar · **Focus:** describing how someone does something · **Role play targets:** very carefully / he speaks slowly / quite well

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — -ly**: Most adverbs add -ly to the describing word: careful becomes carefully, slow becomes slowly. Words ending in -y change to -ily: easy becomes easily. _Examples:_ He drives very carefully. / She speaks slowly and clearly.
- **p2 — fast, hard, well**: 'Fast' and 'hard' do not change; 'good' becomes 'well'. 'Hardly' is a different word and does not mean 'with effort'. _Examples:_ He works hard and drives fast. / She speaks English quite well.
- **p3 — where the adverb goes**: The adverb of manner usually comes after the verb or after the object. It does not go between the verb and its object. _Examples:_ She opened the door quietly. / He answered the question politely.

**Practice block (12 items)**

## 23. Which One Would You Pick?  `a2-which-one-would-you-pick`

**Type:** practice · **Focus:** comparing two real options and explaining your choice · **Role play targets:** I'd rather / it depends on / in my opinion

**Teaching points**

- **p1 — I'd rather**: 'I'd rather' plus the base verb states your choice without sounding pushy. Add 'than' to name the option you are rejecting. _Examples:_ I'd rather take the first one. / I'd rather wait than pay that much.
- **p2 — it depends on**: 'It depends on' is followed by a noun or by a question word. It says your answer would change if the situation changed. _Examples:_ It depends on the price. / It depends on how much time you have.
- **p3 — in my opinion**: 'In my opinion' marks the next sentence as your view, not a fact. It usually stands at the start, followed by a comma. _Examples:_ In my opinion, the second one is safer. / In my opinion, it is worth waiting.

**Practice block (11 items)**

1. `p1` _Fill the gap_ — I'd rather take the train ___ fly. → **✔ than** · to · from

3. `p1` _Say this sentence_ — 🎤 **I'd rather take the first one.**
4. `p2` _Fill the gap_ — It depends ___ the traffic. → **✔ on** · of · from
5. `p2` _Complete the chat_ — THEM: Bus or taxi? / YOU: ___ → **✔ It depends on the weather.** · It depends of the weather. · It depends the weather.
6. `p2` _Listen and arrange_ — 🔊 **It depends on how much time you have.** (extra tiles: of)
7. `p3` _Fill the gap_ — ___ my opinion, the second one is safer. → **✔ In** · On · For
8. `p3` _Complete the chat_ — THEM: Is the city safe at night? / YOU: ___ → **✔ In my opinion, it's quite safe.** · On my opinion, it's quite safe. · In mine opinion, it's quite safe.
